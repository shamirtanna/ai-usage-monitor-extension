AI Usage Monitor and Skill Protection

A quick guide to how it reads your AI prompts: what it measures, and why.

The idea: AI is great at giving answers, but if you hand it the thinking too, the underlying skill quietly fades (deliberate-practice and testing-effect research; Ericsson; Roediger & Karpicke; Bjork). This tool reads each prompt you send to an AI, works out whether it's a moment where your own thinking matters, and only then nudges you to bring it first.

1. What you're asking AI to do: the category

Lookup low
Finding a fact or definition. AI is a reference book, fine to delegate.
"What's the difference between OKRs and KPIs?"
Draft (execution) low
The thinking is done; AI is the typist (format, fix, shorten).
"Format this into a clean bulleted table."
Draft (thinking) high
The content/argument IS the work (persuade, propose, frame). Your reasoning is the point.
"Write a memo to persuade my VP to fund the pilot."
Think high
A judgment, decision, or strategy call, where the thinking itself matters most.
"Should we move our product from B2C to B2B?"

A fifth bucket, Undetermined, covers operational chatter ("yes", "continue", "thanks"): not a real work task, never nudged, excluded from your stats.

2. Did you bring your own thinking: the protective behaviors

These are the things that keep the skill yours. They fall on two spines:

Spine 1: Test yourself (protect the skill)

  • Hypothesis first: offer your own answer/view before asking.
  • Questioned the output: push back on or doubt the AI's answer.
  • Went deeper: ask why / for the root cause.
  • Challenged the source: ask for evidence or reliability.
  • Active recall: try to remember before looking it up.
  • Hypothesized the answer: guess before checking.
  • Iterated on the output: refine and push a prior answer further.

Spine 2: Bring what AI can't (protect your unique value)

  • Human experience: reason from your own past / expertise.
  • Combined ideas: connect it to another idea of yours.
  • Combined domains / analogy: apply a lesson from a different field.
  • Extended from the source: do something with a fact, not just take it.
  • Brought content / outline: supply your own points or structure.

3. When it nudges you: the risk

Risk is category + whether you brought reasoning:

  • high High-stakes task (Think / Draft-thinking) with no protective behavior. It nudges.
  • medium Same task, but you brought your own thinking. No nudge.
  • low Lookup, execution, or operational. Never nudges.

The nudge fires before the prompt is sent, while the thinking is still yours to do, and asks you to bring a hypothesis, then asks the AI to challenge it rather than just agree.

4. How it decides (heuristics first, AI for the hard calls)

Fast keyword heuristics handle the clear cases instantly and for free. Only when a prompt is ambiguous, or looks high-risk (about to nudge), does it ask an LLM to double-check by meaning, catching reasoning phrased in ways keywords miss (e.g. "back when I ran a similar launch" = human experience). The LLM informs the judgment; the risk rules stay fixed.

5. FAQ: when (and why) you get nudged

When exactly does it nudge me?

Only when a prompt is high-risk: a Think or Draft-thinking task where you brought no reasoning of your own. That's the one moment where handing off the thinking costs you the skill. Lookups, formatting, and prompts where you already showed your thinking are left alone.

Why did it nudge me on something I'd already thought about?

The fast heuristic only spots reasoning phrased in familiar ways. If your thinking was worded differently, it can miss it, which is why high-risk prompts get a second, meaning-based check. If it still nudged you, you can just skip; skipping is one click and always sends your prompt as-is.

Why didn't it nudge me on a big decision?

Two likely reasons: you already brought a hypothesis, experience, or pushback (so it counts you as engaged), or the prompt read as a simple lookup. It errs toward not interrupting; over-nudging kills the habit.

Does it change or send my prompt for me?

Never silently. If you write a hypothesis in the nudge, it's added to your prompt (you can see it), along with a note asking the AI to challenge your thinking rather than just agree. Skip, and your original goes exactly as typed.

Can I turn it off?

Yes. Nudges and tracking can each be toggled off. It's a practice aid, not a gate.

6. The research

The tool draws on findings in learning science:

  • Testing effect / retrieval practice: actively retrieving or generating an answer strengthens memory far more than passively reading one. (Roediger & Karpicke, Science, 2008.)
  • Desirable difficulties: a bit of effortful struggle during learning slows you down but builds durable, transferable skill. (Bjork; Soderstrom & Bjork, 2015.)
  • Deliberate practice: skill grows from focused, effortful reps, not passive exposure. (Ericsson.)
  • Spacing effect: retrieval is worth more the longer since you last touched the material; the basis of the "recall what you looked up" check. (Ebbinghaus; Cepeda et al., 2006.)
  • Consider-the-opposite: deliberately generating alternatives reduces confirmation bias and overconfidence; why the nudge asks the AI to challenge you and offer other angles. (Lord, Lepper & Preston, 1984.)
  • LLM sycophancy: a documented tendency of AI assistants to agree with the user; the reason bringing a hypothesis alone isn't enough, and we explicitly ask for pushback.
Leading indicators only: this measures behaviors, not a direct proof of skill change. Built as a personal experiment in protecting thinking while using AI.
